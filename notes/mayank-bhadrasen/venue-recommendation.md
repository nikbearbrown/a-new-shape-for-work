## 1. Does Northeastern pay the full fee?

Yes, for all three. I searched each journal in SciFree, the library's journal search, on November 25, 2026.

| Journal | Who pays | Publishing model | Licence shown |
|---|---|---|---|
| Big Data & Society | Library (full fee) | Open access | CC BY |
| New Technology, Work and Employment | Library (full fee) | Hybrid | Multiple licences |
| Work and Occupations | Library (full fee) | Hybrid | CC BY |

- **No cap:** the library's open access page lists no limit for SAGE or Wiley. It says SAGE fees are "fully covered" in "Unlimited Gold OA and Unlimited Hybrid OA journals", and Wiley hybrid and open access journals are "fully covered". The publishers with a limit are Elsevier (26 articles for 2026), Taylor & Francis (5 for 2026) and the American Chemical Society.
- **Two conditions from SciFree:** submit with the Northeastern email, and choose the CC BY licence.
- **Licence:** *Big Data & Society*'s default licence is CC BY-NC, but its guidelines say other licences are available on request. We would need to ask for CC BY.
- **Article types:** SciFree confirms the journal is covered but does not list article types. If we choose a short format (a commentary or research note), we should confirm with the library that it is covered.

## 4. Why Big Data & Society

1. **The current draft fits.** A research article can be up to 10,000 words, so the paper has room to grow as reviews and new evidence come in. The other two need at least 6,000 or 10,000 words for a full article.
2. **It is fully open access and fully paid for,** with no cap. Anyone can read the paper for free as soon as it is published.
3. **It suits our methods.** The journal asks for "experiments with innovative methods", and it can host datasets, images and videos next to the article, which suits the dashboard.
4. **What we would need to add:** I found no paper in the journal built on job ads or O*NET, and its papers on AI and work are mostly critical or qualitative. The journal asks authors to reflect on "how societies are represented". So the paper would need a section on what each data source (O*NET, job ads, Claude usage) can and cannot tell us about work.

## 5. Why New Technology, Work and Employment is the runner-up

- **The closest topic:** technology and work is exactly what it covers. It also decides quickly (17 days to a first decision).
- **Hard to get into:** it accepts 7% of papers.
- **Length:** a full original paper needs at least 6,000 words. A 5,000-word research note is possible, but the guidelines say notes are rejected if they are "underdeveloped versions of a standard article" or "do not raise substantive theoretical/policy issues".
- **Extra requirements:** the cover letter must explain how the paper relates to debates in the social sciences and how it treats technology.

*Work and Occupations* has the closest past paper (Palmedo et al. 2026 uses job postings) and a current special issue on AI and work. Its 10,000-word minimum makes it the biggest stretch for now.

## 6. Open question: the public draft

All three journals use double-anonymized review, so reviewers must not know who wrote the paper. Our draft is public on GitHub with our names on it.

All three accept papers that were first posted on a preprint server, if:
- we give the preprint's DOI when we submit;
- we don't update the preprint while the paper is under review;
- we link the preprint to the published version afterwards.

None of them mentions GitHub.
